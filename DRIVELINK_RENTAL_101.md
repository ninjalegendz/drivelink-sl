# Vehicle Rental 101

## A zero-assumption introduction for the founder of DriveLink

**Sri Lanka edition**  
**Updated:** 13 July 2026

> This is an educational guide to how vehicle rentals work in practice and how to think about the product. It is not legal, insurance, tax, or financial advice. A Sri Lankan lawyer, insurer/broker, accountant, and the relevant authorities must confirm the final rules used by DriveLink.

---

## 1. Start with the simplest possible idea

A vehicle rental is temporary possession.

One person or business owns or controls a vehicle. Another person pays for permission to use it for an agreed period under agreed conditions. At the end, the second person returns the vehicle.

That simple sentence creates five big questions:

1. **Identity:** Who are the two people?
2. **Authority:** Does the owner have the right to rent this vehicle, and may the renter legally drive it?
3. **Agreement:** Exactly what did each person promise?
4. **Condition:** What was the vehicle like before and after the rental?
5. **Money:** What was paid, what must be returned, and who pays when something goes wrong?

Almost every rental dispute is one of those five questions with missing or conflicting evidence.

DriveLink is not fundamentally a vehicle website. It is a system for answering those five questions reliably.

---

## 2. The people in a rental

### Renter or hirer

The person receiving temporary use of the vehicle.

The renter may be:

- A Sri Lankan resident renting for a few days
- A tourist using a foreign licence or permit
- A business employee using a company rental
- A long-term renter paying monthly
- A person hiring a vehicle together with a driver

The person booking and the person driving are not always the same. That is why **additional drivers** must be declared and checked.

### Vehicle owner

The legal owner shown by the vehicle's registration records, or a person/business with legal authority to rent it.

An owner can be:

- An individual with one car
- A small operator with three or four vehicles
- A registered rent-a-car business
- A fleet owner
- A person whose vehicle is financed or leased, subject to their finance agreement

Owning a car does not automatically mean every insurer, finance company, or contract permits renting it to strangers.

### Rental Page

DriveLink's public operating identity for the supplier.

A Rental Page is not the vehicle and it is not the owner's personal identity. It is the public storefront that lists vehicles, receives requests, signs as the owner/business, communicates with renters, and receives reviews.

One person may operate multiple pages, but each booking must clearly identify:

- The page the renter dealt with
- The real legal person or business behind it
- The exact vehicle
- The person authorized to perform handover

### Driver

The person physically driving.

For self-drive rentals, this is normally the renter or a declared additional driver. For with-driver rentals, the driver is supplied by the Rental Page.

### Platform

DriveLink introduces the parties and keeps the transaction record. Its exact responsibility depends on its business model.

A platform can be:

- **A directory:** it publishes advertisements and sends leads
- **A marketplace:** it manages requests, communication, records, and reviews
- **A payment marketplace:** it also collects and distributes money
- **A managed rental operator:** it controls pricing, approval, insurance, handover, support, and claims

DriveLink's launch blueprint is primarily the second model: a marketplace and trusted record keeper, while rental money and deposits move directly between the parties.

## 3. The main types of rental

### Self-drive

The renter takes the vehicle without a supplied driver.

This creates the most owner risk because the vehicle leaves the owner's physical control. It requires checking the driver's licence class, identity, insurance eligibility, permitted use, and condition evidence.

### With driver or chauffeur-driven

The Rental Page supplies both vehicle and driver.

The renter buys transport rather than independent control of the car. The renter normally does not need a driving licence, but the platform still needs clear pricing for driver hours, overtime, meals or overnight allowance, accommodation, fuel, parking, tolls, and route limits.

“With driver” is not automatically simpler. It changes the disputes from “who damaged the car?” to questions such as overtime, route, driver conduct, waiting time, and expenses.

### Short-term rental

Usually hours, days, or a few weeks. Handover and return happen frequently, so availability, daily pricing, cleaning, deposits, and inspections matter heavily.

### Long-term rental

Usually one or more months.

Long-term rental needs additional clarity:

- Monthly included kilometres
- Service and maintenance responsibility
- Tyres, battery, oil and routine wear
- Replacement vehicle during repairs
- Payment date each month
- Early termination
- Who keeps original documents
- Periodic condition inspections
- Insurance renewal during the term
- Revenue licence and emission-test renewal
- Whether the renter may take the vehicle outside agreed areas

A monthly rate is not simply “daily price multiplied by 30.” The owner usually discounts the rate because occupancy is guaranteed and repeated handovers are avoided.

### Delivery rental

The vehicle is taken to the renter rather than collected at its normal location. The quote must identify delivery and collection fees separately, who bears delay risk, and when the rental clock starts.

### Airport rental

Airport service may include flight tracking, waiting time, parking, delivery, permit assistance, and late-night handover. It should not be treated as a normal city pickup with a different address.

## 4. What is actually being sold?

The renter is not buying only kilometres or days. They are buying a package of permissions and obligations.

That package includes:

- The right to possess/use one identified vehicle
- A start and end date/time
- A pickup and return location
- A permitted driver or drivers
- A permitted geographic/use scope
- A mileage allowance
- A fuel policy
- Support if the vehicle breaks down
- An insurance arrangement
- Rules for cancellation, lateness, accidents, damage, fines, cleaning, and deposits

If any one of those is vague, the cheaper-looking rental may become the most expensive one.

## 5. The money, one term at a time

### Daily, weekly, or monthly rental rate

The base price for use of the vehicle. It normally does not automatically include every other cost.

### Booking fee

Money paid by the renter to the platform for arranging or processing a booking. It is different from the rental price paid to the owner.

### Commission or success fee

Money charged to the owner/page because the platform produced a completed booking.

A 10% commission on a Rs 50,000 rental is Rs 5,000. A flat success fee might instead be Rs 500 regardless of rental value.

### Security deposit

Money temporarily held to protect against defined obligations such as damage, missing fuel, excess kilometres, fines, late return, or missing accessories.

The deposit is **not rental income** and it is not automatically a damage payment. It remains the renter's money unless a contractually valid deduction is established.

Every deposit needs:

- Exact amount fixed before confirmation
- Who received it
- Payment method and timestamp
- What deductions are allowed
- Evidence required for a deduction
- Maximum decision/return period
- Process for partial return
- Renter acknowledgement of the returned amount
- Dispute path

### Insurance excess or deductible

The amount that may remain payable by the insured/renter before or alongside an insurer's settlement, subject to the policy and claim.

An excess of Rs 50,000 does **not** necessarily mean every scratch costs Rs 50,000. It also does not mean the renter's maximum liability is always Rs 50,000. The policy, cause, exclusions, agreement, evidence, and actual loss matter.

### Mileage allowance

The distance included in the price.

Example: 100 km per day for four days gives 400 included kilometres. If the vehicle travels 520 km and the extra rate is Rs 35/km:

```text
Extra distance: 520 - 400 = 120 km
Extra charge:   120 x Rs 35 = Rs 4,200
```

This requires trustworthy pickup and return odometer photographs.

### Fuel policy

The expected fuel level at return. Common versions include same-to-same and full-to-full.

A clear fuel record needs both a gauge photo and a stated policy. “Approximately half” is an invitation to argue.

### Delivery, driver, toll, parking, and overnight charges

These should be separate structured price items, not surprises in chat.

### Late fee

The charge after an agreed grace period. It must not accidentally stack an hourly charge, a complete extra rental day, and a penalty for the same period unless the agreement clearly and lawfully intends that result.

### Cleaning fee

A charge for cleaning beyond ordinary return condition. Normal dust from ordinary use is different from smoking, stains, excessive mud, pet hair, or biohazards. Evidence and a cap help prevent abuse.

### Loss of use

Claimed income lost while a damaged vehicle cannot be rented.

This is easy to exaggerate. A fair system needs proof of repair downtime, a reasonable daily basis, evidence of demand where relevant, and a contractual cap reviewed by a lawyer.

## 6. A complete worked example

Assume Nimal books a self-drive car for four 24-hour days.

```text
Daily rental:                Rs 8,000 x 4 = Rs 32,000
Delivery:                                  Rs  3,000
Security deposit:                          Rs 50,000
Included distance:                         400 km
Extra distance after return:               120 km
Extra-km charge:             Rs 35 x 120 = Rs  4,200
Fuel shortage supported by receipt:        Rs  2,000
```

At confirmation/handover, Nimal may transfer Rs 85,000: Rs 35,000 rental/delivery plus Rs 50,000 deposit.

At return, the legitimate additional charge is Rs 6,200. If both parties agree and there is no other claim:

```text
Deposit received:                          Rs 50,000
Agreed deductions:                         Rs  6,200
Deposit returned:                          Rs 43,800
Final cost of rental:                      Rs 41,200
```

The Rs 50,000 deposit was not itself part of the final rental cost. DriveLink should record each movement and acknowledgement even when it does not process the payment.

## 7. Insurance without the confusing sales language

### Third-party insurance

At a basic level, this concerns liability to other people/property rather than fully repairing the rented vehicle itself. It is not equivalent to comprehensive rental protection.

### Comprehensive insurance

Broader cover that may include damage to the insured vehicle, but only under the policy's terms, permitted use, drivers, exclusions, excesses, reporting duties, and endorsements.

### Private-use cover versus rental/hire use

This is one of the most important ideas in the whole business.

An ordinary private motor policy may not cover commercial self-drive rental. The correct permission cannot be inferred merely from a certificate saying “comprehensive.” The policy schedule, permitted-use clause, and rental/hirer-driving endorsement must be confirmed with the insurer.

Sri Lanka Insurance's published motor wording contains a specific “Hire Vehicles - Hirer Driving” clause. It expects a hire contract and a duly licensed driver, and also contains exclusions that matter to owner risk. That illustrates why DriveLink must display facts from the actual policy rather than inventing a universal “insured” badge. [Sri Lanka Insurance motor policy wording](https://www.srilankainsurance.com/images/pdf/motor-english-policy-book-2023.pdf)

### What insurance does not magically solve

An insurer can reject or reduce a claim because of issues such as:

- Wrong class of use
- Unlicensed or undeclared driver
- Alcohol/drug exclusions
- Late or missing notification
- No required police report
- Prohibited use
- Fraud or misrepresentation
- Wear, mechanical breakdown, or pre-existing damage
- Theft/conversion exclusions involving a hirer
- Policy lapse or unpaid premium
- Underinsurance

The exact answer comes from that vehicle's policy and insurer, not from generic platform text.

### DriveLink's correct insurance posture

DriveLink can say:

- Which insurer/policy document was reviewed
- Whether the submitted policy describes rental/hire use
- Policy and expiry dates
- Stated excess
- Whether the page uploaded updated evidence

DriveLink should not say:

- “Every accident is covered”
- “DriveLink guarantees this claim”
- “The renter can never owe more than the excess”
- “Comprehensive means rental use is covered”

The badge should mean **document reviewed**, not **future claim guaranteed**.

## 8. Licences and tourist permits

### Sri Lankan drivers

The licence must be valid, belong to the actual driver, and cover the relevant vehicle class. The Department of Motor Traffic states that a person must be 18 to face the practical test for a light-vehicle licence. A rental company may impose a higher minimum age or minimum driving experience as a risk/insurance rule. [Department of Motor Traffic](https://dmt.gov.lk/index.php?id=48&lang=en&option=com_content&view=article)

### Foreign drivers

Do not reduce this to “tourist uploaded a foreign licence.”

Sri Lanka Police stated in February 2026 that owners/operators must verify an appropriate valid permit before providing vehicles to foreign tourists. Its notice describes acceptable routes including a relevant International Driving Permit, a Sri Lankan permit, or an official conversion/translation path, and warns that both driver and owner can face consequences when an unlicensed person is allowed to drive. [Sri Lanka Police notice](https://www.police.lk/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Media-on-2026.02.19-at-1200-_compressed.pdf)

The Motor Traffic Act also recognizes qualifying International Driving Permits under stated conditions, including the 1968 Vienna Convention route. The exact document path depends on the visitor and permit. [Motor Traffic (Amendment) Act, No. 8 of 2009](https://www.dmt.gov.lk/images/PDF/Downloads/act_no_08of2009_en.pdf)

The owner must inspect the **original valid document** at handover. A platform upload helps preparation but is not the physical handover check.

### Recognition Permit as a product

AA Ceylon publishes a Recognition Permit service, and ROFI already markets permit processing as a paid tourist service. This can become a DriveLink partner service later, but booking eligibility and the owner's original-document check must remain separate from selling assistance. [AA Ceylon](https://aaceylon.lk/membership/), [ROFI permit service](https://www.rofi.lk/recognition-permit)

## 9. The vehicle documents

At minimum, the owner and platform should understand:

### Certificate of Registration (CR/book)

Evidence concerning the vehicle's registered identity and ownership. Plate, chassis/VIN, engine information, and registered owner should match the listing and physical vehicle.

### Revenue licence

Evidence that the vehicle is currently licensed for road use, subject to the applicable process.

### Insurance certificate and policy schedule

Evidence of current insurance, but the use/endorsement and exclusions need review rather than relying on the word “comprehensive.”

### Emission certificate

Current evidence where applicable.

### Fitness/mechanical condition

A valid piece of paper does not prove tyres, lights, brakes, wipers, seatbelts, fluid levels, spare wheel, and warning lights are safe today.

The 2026 Police warning specifically emphasized valid licence, insurance, revenue/emission/fitness documents and mechanical condition for vehicles supplied to foreign visitors. [Sri Lanka Police notice](https://www.police.lk/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Media-on-2026.02.19-at-1200-_compressed.pdf)

## 10. The rental journey from beginning to end

### Stage 1: Discovery

The renter searches by location, dates, time, vehicle type, mode, price, seats, insurance information, mileage, delivery, and page reputation.

No private contact or identity document is needed merely to browse.

### Stage 2: Quote

The renter sees a complete price estimate and rules before requesting:

- Base rental
- Mode and driver costs
- Delivery/collection
- Included distance and extra rate
- Deposit
- Fuel policy
- Cancellation rules
- Late-return rules
- Insurance facts/excess
- Required licence/permit

### Stage 3: Booking request

The renter asks the page to reserve the vehicle for a particular period. A request is not yet proof that the rental will happen.

The page reviews relevant trust information: verified identity status, licence/permit readiness for self-drive, history, dates, mode, and special requests.

### Stage 4: Acceptance and terms snapshot

The page accepts. At that moment DriveLink freezes the offered vehicle, dates, price, deposit, rules, and cancellation terms.

The owner cannot silently raise the deposit after seeing the renter.

### Stage 5: Consent-controlled documents

Only now, and for a stated booking purpose, the renter may grant the page access to necessary identity/licence documents.

Access should be limited, watermarked, and logged. Consent to future viewing is different from legally justified retention of a past transaction record.

### Stage 6: Agreement

Both parties accept one immutable agreement identifying:

- Legal renter and authorized drivers
- Legal owner/business and Rental Page
- Exact vehicle
- Rental mode
- Dates, times, locations
- Price and deposit
- Rules and insurance facts
- Accident/breakdown procedure
- Late/return/claim process
- Signatures/acceptance timestamps

### Stage 7: Payment record

During DriveLink's direct-payment launch, the parties pay each other. DriveLink records declarations/receipts and acknowledgements but does not pretend it processed the money.

### Stage 8: Pickup inspection

The page records:

- Plate and vehicle match
- Odometer
- Fuel gauge
- Front, rear, left, right
- Wheels/tyres where useful
- Interior and dashboard
- Existing damage close-ups
- Accessories/documents/keys
- Warning lights and notes

The renter accepts or reports a difference. Only after this and the agreement does the rental become **active**.

### Stage 9: During the rental

Messages, extensions, breakdowns, accidents, driver changes, and incidents remain tied to the booking.

An extension is not merely a chat message. It changes dates, price, availability, terms, and possibly insurance, so both parties must accept an amendment.

### Stage 10: Return inspection

Repeat the same evidence and compare it side by side against pickup.

### Stage 11: Settlement

Record mileage, fuel, lateness, cleaning, supported claims, and deposit return. Both sides confirm what was paid/returned or open a dispute.

### Stage 12: Completion and review

Only a genuinely concluded booking can create a public review. The review belongs to the Rental Page, while renter reliability remains a private operational signal.

## 11. Why inspections matter so much

Imagine the renter returns a car with a scratch on the rear bumper.

There are four possibilities:

1. The scratch existed before pickup.
2. The renter caused it.
3. Someone else caused it while the renter possessed the car.
4. The owner caused or discovered it after return.

Without timestamped pickup and return photographs, both people are mostly telling stories.

An inspection is not a gallery of random photos. It is a repeatable comparison protocol.

Good evidence is:

- Taken at the correct stage
- Connected to the correct booking and vehicle
- Submitted by an identified person
- Timestamped
- Organized by required angle/field
- Acknowledged or disputed by the other party
- Immutable after submission

That is why “upload at least four photos” is not enough.

## 12. Common scenarios, explained plainly

### Scenario A: Normal rental

The parties sign, inspect, hand over, communicate, inspect return, settle charges, return deposit, and review. This is the happy path the product should make very fast.

### Scenario B: Fake or misleading listing

The renter pays or travels to pickup and discovers the vehicle does not exist, belongs to someone else, or is materially different.

Useful controls:

- Admin review before publication
- Plate/CR/page ownership checks
- Current real photographs
- Page identity
- Payment/contact gating
- Plate-match check at pickup
- Report listing action

### Scenario C: Owner cancels late

The renter arranged a trip, hotel, or flight and the owner cancels the day before.

Useful controls:

- Immediate notice
- Alternative vehicle assistance
- Full return of anything paid where applicable
- Owner cancellation strike
- Factual recent-cancellation signal
- Ranking reduction for repeated behavior

### Scenario D: Renter cancels

The owner may have rejected other bookings and prepared/delivered the vehicle. A fair cancellation ladder becomes stricter as pickup approaches and must be visible before confirmation.

### Scenario E: Breakdown without renter fault

The car develops a mechanical problem.

Questions:

- Is it safe to continue?
- Who arranges towing/repair?
- Is a replacement vehicle available?
- Who pays accommodation/transport?
- Does the rental clock pause?
- Is the unused period refunded?

These answers belong in the agreement/protocol, not improvised during distress.

### Scenario F: Accident

First priorities are human safety and lawful reporting, not arguing over the deposit.

The workflow should surface the insurer/owner emergency instructions, location, photographs, third-party/witness information, police-report requirements, and a rule not to admit or privately settle liability contrary to insurer instructions. The Rent A Car Association's renter guide similarly tells renters to clarify accident, assessor, and police-report procedures. [RACA renter guide](https://www.raca.lk/guide-to-renting-a-vehicle-in-sri-lanka/)

### Scenario G: New damage at return

The page files a structured claim linked to return inspection, description, estimate, and amount. The renter accepts or disputes. Deposit deduction does not become automatically correct just because the owner typed a number.

### Scenario H: Deposit not returned

The page delays or invents deductions.

Useful controls:

- Deposit amount locked in agreement
- Received acknowledgement
- Return deadline
- Itemized deduction with evidence
- Partial-return reason
- Renter acknowledgement/dispute
- Page reliability consequence

### Scenario I: Late return

Start with a grace period and communication. Then apply the recorded fee. At a serious threshold, escalate account/owner alerts. At 24+ hours and unreachable, preserve evidence and support lawful police reporting rather than auto-completing the booking.

### Scenario J: Vehicle is not returned

This is the owner's nightmare.

DriveLink's useful evidence pack would contain:

- Booking/agreement
- Verified renter identity
- Licence/permit record
- Vehicle identity
- Pickup inspection
- Payment/deposit record
- Messages/contact attempts
- Scheduled return and overdue timestamps
- Last renter actions and incident report

DriveLink does not declare someone criminal. It preserves factual evidence for the owner, police, insurer, and legal process.

### Scenario K: Fine arrives after return

Some notices arrive later. The agreement needs a reasonable claim window, proof linking the time/vehicle, notice upload, and renter response. The owner should not have unlimited years to produce an unexplained fine.

### Scenario L: Undeclared additional driver

The renter lets a friend drive. The friend may be unlicensed, outside insurance terms, or below a contractual age limit.

The platform must make “authorized drivers only” prominent and support adding a verified driver through an accepted amendment.

### Scenario M: Off-platform deal

The parties exchange WhatsApp numbers early and finish privately.

They may save a fee, but DriveLink loses the booking record, agreement, inspection, support context, review, monetization, and ability to help. This is why contact unlock and in-app communication are strategic, not cosmetic.

## 13. The main scams and abuses

### Against renters

- Fake vehicle/listing
- Vehicle switched at pickup
- Hidden deposit/rules
- Existing damage blamed on renter
- Deposit withheld without evidence
- Fake insurance claim
- Owner cancellation/no-show
- Unsafe or poorly maintained vehicle
- Private insurance presented as rental cover

### Against owners

- Fake/stolen identity
- Forged licence/permit
- Vehicle not returned
- Vehicle pawned, sold, stripped, or used for crime
- Unauthorized driver
- Mileage/odometer manipulation
- Accident concealed
- Charge/deposit refusal
- Repeated low-intent requests

### Against the platform

- Parties leave to avoid fees
- Fake reviews/trips
- Self-verification or moderator bypass
- Storage abuse
- Multiple accounts after blacklisting
- Fraudulent reports against competitors
- Page impersonation

Trust must protect all three sides. A system that treats every renter as dangerous will have no demand. A system that treats every owner as trustworthy will harm renters.

## 14. The “yin-yangs”: every good decision has a cost

### Low-friction listings versus fake inventory

Fewer required documents bring more owners. They also make fake/unsafe listings easier. The balance is staged verification plus mandatory admin review before publication.

### Privacy versus owner confidence

Owners genuinely need to know who takes a valuable vehicle. Renters should not distribute permanent NIC copies to strangers. The balance is verified status before acceptance and narrow, consented, logged document access afterward.

### Instant booking versus owner control

Instant booking improves conversion. Small owners may not maintain accurate calendars or may need to assess renters. The balance at launch is request-to-book with fast response expectations; instant booking comes only for pages/inventory that can honor it reliably.

### Multiple requests versus date certainty

Allowing several requests gives owners choice but leaves renters uncertain. Letting the first request block dates lets one weak lead monopolize inventory. DriveLink must explicitly choose and explain its hold/stacking model.

### Direct payment versus platform protection

Direct payment reduces DriveLink's custody, refund, chargeback, VAT/accounting, and deposit burden. It also gives the platform less control when payment disputes happen. The balance is direct money movement with structured receipts, two-sided acknowledgements, and evidence.

### Platform payments versus operational/legal burden

Collecting the full amount improves convenience and fee collection. It also means reconciliation, refunds, deposits, chargebacks, payout delays, fraud, tax/accounting, and greater user expectations.

ROFI takes the managed route: its published terms say the platform collects full payment and a refundable deposit. RentnRide says it collects rental payment and pays owners weekly, while deposits remain direct. DriveLink is choosing the lower-custody launch route. None is free of tradeoffs. [ROFI terms](https://www.rofi.lk/terms-and-conditions), [RentnRide terms](https://www.rentnride.lk/terms-conditions)

### Standard terms versus owner flexibility

Standard terms make quotes and agreements understandable. Owners have genuinely different vehicles and costs. The balance is sensible defaults plus bounded adjustments, not unlimited free text.

### Strong penalties versus honest mistakes

Cancellation and late-return consequences deter abuse. Emergencies and breakdowns happen. The balance is factual evidence, reason categories, proportional strikes, review/appeal, and decay rather than permanent punishment for one event.

### Permanent evidence versus data minimization

Disputes may emerge after completion. Identity and licence documents are highly sensitive. The balance is retaining only what has a defined lawful purpose, cutting off unnecessary viewing, documenting retention periods, and separating evidence records from reusable raw documents.

### Public reviews versus retaliation

Reviews help renters choose pages. Publicly rating renters can become coercive or discriminatory. The balance is public Page reviews from completed bookings and private factual renter reliability/history for legitimate booking assessment.

### Free listings versus marketplace quality

Free supply accelerates inventory but encourages abandoned/duplicate listings. The balance is free publication with review, expiry/reconfirmation, response requirements, duplicate detection, and ranking based on real completed service.

### Growth versus geographic density

“Islandwide” sounds large. Ten vehicles scattered over ten districts are less useful than ten suitable vehicles in one active area. Marketplace quality comes from local density, not the size of the map.

## 15. Why long-term rental feels different

Since you have experienced long-term rental, you saw only one slice of the system.

In long-term rental:

- The same vehicle remains with one renter
- Monthly payment becomes routine
- Small condition changes accumulate gradually
- Maintenance/service deadlines occur during possession
- Deposit may remain tied up for months
- Relationship and WhatsApp communication replace repeated app actions
- Availability/search matters once, while ongoing account management matters every month

DriveLink should eventually support long-term rentals as a sequence:

1. Initial agreement and inspection
2. Monthly invoice/payment acknowledgement
3. Periodic odometer/condition check
4. Maintenance events
5. Extension/amendment
6. Final return and settlement

Trying to force a six-month rental into one ordinary daily booking produces poor records.

## 16. What DriveLink is responsible for, even without holding money

“We are only a platform” is not a magic sentence.

DriveLink chooses what it publishes, verifies, ranks, stores, shares, automates, and promises. It must accurately describe those actions.

At minimum it should own the quality of:

- Account authentication
- Page ownership and moderation
- Listing publication controls
- Booking authorization and state
- Terms snapshots and agreements
- Inspection/evidence integrity
- Document privacy and access logs
- Message records
- Review eligibility
- Report/appeal process
- Notifications it claims were sent
- Data retention/deletion

It should not claim to decide:

- Criminal guilt
- Insurance claim coverage
- Final legal liability
- Authenticity beyond the verification actually performed
- That a vehicle is mechanically safe forever

## 17. Personal data is not ordinary content

DriveLink handles names, addresses, phone numbers, NIC/passport/licence images, faces, booking movements, messages, ratings, and possible accusations. That is a high-risk dataset.

Sri Lanka's PDPA has operational provisions in force, and the Data Protection Authority regulates personal-data processing. The Act requires a lawful basis; where consent is used, it must be demonstrable, clearly presented, and withdrawable subject to the Act. Collection notices should identify purpose, recipients, retention, rights, complaint route, and other required information. [Data Protection Authority](https://www.dpa.gov.lk/est.php), [Personal Data Protection Act](https://www.dpa.gov.lk/acts/Data%20Protection%20Act%20SL%20-%20English%20%282%29.pdf)

Plain-English product consequences:

- Do not make identity documents public
- Do not collect a document merely because it may be useful someday
- Tell the user why it is collected
- Tell them who can see it
- Log actual access
- Permit appropriate withdrawal/restriction
- Retain evidence under a defined policy, not forever by accident
- Protect raw files from caches and direct links
- Provide correction, deletion/restriction, and appeal workflows
- Do not promise deletion/access behavior the code does not perform

## 18. Tax and money custody in one page

Revenue earned by DriveLink is not the same as all rental money passing through its bank account.

Example:

- Renter pays Rs 100,000 rental
- DriveLink keeps Rs 5,000 fee
- Owner receives Rs 95,000

Even though DriveLink economically earned Rs 5,000, processing Rs 100,000 creates reconciliation, payout, refund, accounting, fraud, and contractual responsibilities that do not exist in the same way when renter pays owner directly and DriveLink separately invoices Rs 5,000.

Sri Lanka's Inland Revenue Department currently publishes an 18% standard VAT rate and registration thresholds for taxable supplies, while tax treatment depends on the actual business and current law. Do not design pricing from this paragraph; obtain an accountant's written treatment for booking fees, commissions, owner collections, deposits, and invoices before enabling payment custody. [IRD VAT guidance](https://www.ird.gov.lk/en/Type%20of%20Taxes/SitePages/Value%20Added%20Tax%20%28VAT%29.aspx)

## 19. The competitors as business-model lessons

### ROFI

ROFI appears to be the more mature marketplace. It shows active inventory/trip counts, apps, self-drive/with-driver, permit assistance, and a QR-based trip start/end. It also says it processes full payment and deposit.

Lesson for DriveLink:

- Generic search and “verified marketplace” positioning are not unique
- QR handover is worth adopting as a state trigger
- Tourist permit assistance can be a partner/revenue product
- Full custody creates complexity DriveLink can avoid at launch
- DriveLink needs a sharper evidence/privacy/Page identity advantage

### RentnRide

RentnRide shows structured rules, rental mode, mileage and extra-km terms, insurance/verification labels, and a 10% commission model with platform rental payment and direct deposit.

Lesson for DriveLink:

- Structured data makes listings understandable
- Badges and review numbers destroy trust if they cannot be substantiated
- Never expose an owner's personal email as the public Page identity
- “Instant” must describe real availability and confirmation behavior

## 20. The DriveLink product in one complete sentence

> DriveLink helps a verified renter and an accountable Rental Page agree on one real vehicle, preserve the exact terms and identity permissions, prove its condition at pickup and return, record direct payments and incidents, and leave both parties with a trustworthy booking record.

Search brings people in. That sentence is why they should use DriveLink instead of Facebook or WhatsApp.

## 21. What must be true before the first serious launch

### Security truths

- A user cannot make themselves admin or verified
- A page cannot approve itself or its own vehicle
- One page cannot see or alter another page's records
- Neither party can rewrite the other's evidence
- Raw identity documents cannot be downloaded/cached through a bypass

### Booking truths

- A future confirmed booking is not active before pickup
- Search and detail show the same availability as booking API
- Price and terms cannot silently change after request
- Rental mode and authorized drivers are explicit
- Cancellation actor and consequence are correct

### Trust truths

- Agreement is immutable and available to both parties
- Pickup and return inspections are mandatory
- Every inspection field has a purpose
- Consent and document access are real controls
- Deposit/charge records reconcile
- Claims and disputes cannot disappear
- Reviews require actual completed bookings

### Operational truths

- Notifications report real delivery status
- Overdue jobs run on time
- Admin queues show errors rather than false zeroes
- Reports lead to reviewed decisions and appeals
- Privacy policy matches the system

## 22. Questions to ask whenever someone proposes a feature

For every feature, ask:

1. Who is allowed to do this?
2. At what booking stage?
3. What could they lie about?
4. What evidence proves it happened?
5. Can they change that evidence later?
6. Who receives a notification?
7. What if the notification fails?
8. What money moves, and who holds it?
9. What personal data is exposed?
10. What happens if two actions occur simultaneously?
11. What does the other party see?
12. How can an admin correct a mistake without hiding history?
13. What happens after account deletion?
14. Does the public copy promise more than the code does?
15. How would this look in a police report, insurance claim, or legal dispute?

That checklist is software engineering, rental operations, and trust design at the same time.

## 24. Your mental model from now on

Do not think:

> “The renter selects a car, pays, and drives.”

Think:

> “Two strangers are temporarily transferring control of an expensive, dangerous, regulated asset. The product must make the normal transaction easy while preserving enough truth for the abnormal transaction.”

The normal rental creates growth.

The abnormal rental decides whether people trust DriveLink.
